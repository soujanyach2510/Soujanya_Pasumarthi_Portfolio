AIML-501 · Workshop 5.4

Pre-Trained AI Model Selection: Explainability, Performance & Trade-Offs

A research-based visual decision matrix comparing five pre-trained AI models across Natural Language Processing, Computer Vision, and Tabular Data: BERT, DistilBERT, MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-B0, and TabPFN v2.

The artifact evaluates model size, benchmark performance, inference speed, computational requirements, and explainability techniques to support informed model selection for real-world AI applications.

The analysis demonstrates how application-specific requirements influence model selection and why predictive performance must be evaluated alongside resource constraints, explanation reliability, fairness, and responsible deployment considerations.

Pre-Trained Models Transfer Learning Model Selection NLP Computer Vision Tabular AI Decision Matrix Explainable AI Performance Benchmarking Responsible AI
Objective Develop a structured decision matrix to evaluate pre-trained models based on model size, predictive performance, inference efficiency, interpretability, and application-specific requirements.
Research & Methodology Compare five representative models using academic research and model documentation. Examine parameter counts, task-specific benchmarks, computational constraints, SHAP, Integrated Gradients, and Grad-CAM.
Technical Analysis Evaluate trade-offs between model complexity, accuracy, inference latency, resource consumption, and explanation reliability. Recognize that benchmarks from different domains cannot be directly compared.
Practical Application Illustrate how TabPFN v2 could support healthcare claims anomaly detection, with independent validation, feature attribution, fairness evaluation, and human auditor oversight.
Research Challenges Address differences in benchmark datasets, hardware configurations, inference environments, and explanation fidelity. Published performance does not guarantee equivalent results in production.
Key Learning No single model is optimal for every application. Effective model selection requires balancing business objectives, predictive quality, computational resources, explainability, privacy, and responsible deployment.
